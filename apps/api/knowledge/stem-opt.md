# STEM OPT Extension
Source: DHS STEM Designated Degree Program List, studyinthestates.dhs.gov

The STEM OPT extension adds 24 months of work authorization on top of the
standard 12-month OPT period, for students whose degree qualifies as a STEM
field under DHS rules.

Degree eligibility:
- The student's degree must appear on the DHS STEM Designated Degree Program
  List, based on the Department of Education's CIP (Classification of
  Instructional Programs) taxonomy.
- The core CIP two-digit series covered are: Engineering (14), Biological and
  Biomedical Sciences (26), Mathematics and Statistics (27), and Physical
  Sciences (40), plus additional related fields DHS has designated at the
  four- or six-digit CIP level.
- DHS considers only the student's primary major CIP code for this
  determination, not minor or concentration codes.
- DHS periodically updates this list and publishes changes in the Federal
  Register.

Employer and job requirements:
- The employer must be enrolled in E-Verify.
- There must be a bona fide employer-employee relationship. A student cannot
  sign their own Form I-983 training plan on the employer's behalf.
- Form I-983 (the STEM OPT training plan) must be completed and signed by the
  employer, describing the student's role, goals, and how the employer will
  supervise the training.
- The student's compensation must be commensurate with similarly situated
  U.S. workers at the company.

Unemployment limit: the STEM OPT extension adds to the existing 90-day OPT
unemployment allowance, for a combined cumulative limit of 150 days of
unemployment across the full OPT plus STEM OPT period.

Self-evaluations: the initial self-evaluation on Form I-983 is due at
12 months and the final one at 24 months after the STEM OPT start
date, or when employment ends early. The student and employer both
sign it, and the DSO must receive it within 10 days of the end of the
training period.
