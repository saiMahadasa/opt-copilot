# Curricular Practical Training (CPT)
Source: ICE/SEVP Curricular Practical Training Fact Sheet, studyinthestates.dhs.gov

There are two types of practical training available to F-1 students: curricular
practical training (CPT) and optional practical training (OPT). CPT is an
alternative work/study, internship, cooperative education, or other type of
required internship or practicum that a sponsoring employer offers through
agreements with a student's school.

Key facts about CPT:
- Occurs before a student's program end date on the Form I-20.
- Must relate directly to the student's major area of study and be an integral
  part of the school's established curriculum. It is not a supplemental
  training experience, it must be a required part of the degree program.
- Can be full-time (more than 20 hours per week) or part-time.
- A student's designated school official (DSO) authorizes CPT in SEVIS. The
  authorization prints on the student's Form I-20 for a specific employer and
  a specific period of time. A DSO may not backdate the authorization.
- Students must secure the training opportunity before CPT can be authorized.
- A student may begin CPT only after receiving the DSO-endorsed Form I-20, and
  not before the CPT start date on that form. All work must end by the CPT
  end date.
- CPT authorization is only required when the training takes place inside the
  United States. A summer internship in a student's home country does not
  need CPT authorization.
- CPT requires a signed cooperative agreement or letter from the employer.
- Students can hold more than one CPT authorization at the same time, but a
  separate authorization is needed for each employer and each CPT segment.
- One year or more of full-time CPT eliminates a student's eligibility for
  OPT at the same educational level. Part-time CPT does not affect OPT
  eligibility.

Regulatory basis: 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i).

For case-specific questions, students should contact their school's DSO or
the SEVP Response Center.
